# TD-015 — Decision-making mechanism when team cannot reach consensus

## Status

Proposed

## Context

The project requires a defined mechanism for resolving disagreements when team members cannot reach consensus on a technical or product decision. Without an explicit mechanism, discussions can continue indefinitely, decisions can be made inconsistently, or implementation can become blocked while waiting for agreement.

## Constraints

- The team should attempt to reach consensus before escalating a disagreement.
- Technical decisions should be based on project requirements, constraints, evidence, and trade-offs rather than personal preference alone.
- The process must have a clearly defined final decision-maker.
- Disagreements must not block the project indefinitely.
- Significant decisions should have their rationale documented.
- The mechanism should work for both technical and product-related decisions.
- Reversible decisions should be resolved quickly.
- High-impact or difficult-to-reverse decisions should receive additional discussion before a final decision.
- The process should remain lightweight enough for a small development team.

## Options considered

### Option A: Majority vote


**Pros:**
- Simple and easy to understand.
- Provides a clear mechanism for ending disagreements, that gives every team member an equal formal vote.

---

**Cons:**
- Technical decisions are not necessarily improved by having more votes.
- A majority can select an option despite the person with the most relevant technical expertise disagreeing.
- Encourages framing technical disagreements as preferences rather than evaluating evidence.
- Can become inefficient with a small team when votes are tied (in this case imposible).

### Option B: Consensus with a designated decision owner (DRI)

After attempting to reach consensus by discussing the alternatives, evidence, constraints, and consequences. If consensus cannot be reached within a reasonable amount of time, the designated Directly Responsible Individual (DRI) makes the final decision.

**Pros:**
- Preserves collaborative discussion without requiring unanimous agreement.
- Provides a clear mechanism for resolving deadlocks.
- Allows decisions to be made according to relevant technical or domain expertise.

---

**Cons:**
- The DRI may make a decision that some team members disagree with.
- The process depends on the DRI being willing and able to make decisions.

## Decision

We choose option B, the team will first attempt to reach consensus through discussion. Participants should present relevant requirements, evidence, alternatives, risks, and consequences rather than relying solely on personal preference. If the team cannot reach consensus after reasonable discussion, the DRI for the decision makes the final decision. This fits better with the current team characteristics.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Prevents technical disagreements from indefinitely blocking implementation.
- Preserves collaborative decision-making.
- Encourages evidence-based discussion.

### Negative

- Team members may sometimes need to implement a decision they disagree with.
- The quality of the process depends on the DRI making decisions responsibly.
